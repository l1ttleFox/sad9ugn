In the face of the complex market environment and the in-depth development of globalization, ensuring 
the supply chain’s stability and cost leadership by developing a differentiated dual-source procurement 
strategy is extremely important. The present study contemplates the supply chain as the research object. 
It also considers disruption uncertainty and decision-makers’ risk attitudes. To minimize costs and short- 
ages, we build a dual-objective robust optimization model for a dual-sourcing strategy under interrup- 
tion risk. The authors aim to explore eﬃcient and robust planning under supply disruption uncertainty 
in dual-sourcing strategies by different risk attitudes. The research employs the augmented ε-constraint 
method to solve the proposed model by transforming the model into a single-objective mixed-integer 
programming model. Additionally, our study uses enterprise examples to prove the feasibility and ef- 
fectiveness of the model. Moreover, it conducts risk attitude analysis, dual objective function trade-off
analysis, and superiority analysis of procurement strategies. Finally, we put forward some management 
insights to provide a new decision-making idea for real supply chain enterprises in the case of supply 
interruption risk.

1. Introduction 
The supply chain enterprise’s uncertain internal factors, unpre- 
dictable external events, and unpredictable business environment 
lead to supply interruption, bringing economic losses and dam- 
aging the enterprise’s reputation. Dual-sourcing is a practical and 
cost-effective approach to mitigate interruption risk in the supply 
chain by reducing shortages of minimal economic cost [1] , which 
refers to selecting two suppliers in supply chain procurement to 
share the interruption risk [2] . Decision-makers need to carry out 
relatively effective and stable order planning, which is the key 
to implementing a dual-sourcing strategy under the risk of sup- 
ply interruption. In addition, different risk attitudes of decision- 
makers may affect ordering plans. Therefore, this study explores 
eﬃcient and robust ordering planning in dual-sourcing strategies 
by decision-makers with different risk attitudes under supply dis- 
ruption uncertainty.

Mitigation strategies for supply interruption must establish ﬂex- 
ibility to adapt to supply chain changes; one approach is dual- 
sourcing [2–7] . A well-known example is Nokia and Ericsson up- 
stream supply factory outage incident [8] . After this disruption, 
Nokia designed a quick ﬁx, using a backup supplier to produce the 
chips in just ﬁve days. Meanwhile, Ericsson eliminated backup sup- 
pliers to save costs before this disruption, so it endured a huge loss 
and withdrew from the mobile phone production market. Further, 
this example also tells us how to purchase from the primary and 
backup suppliers, which must be considered in dual-sourcing re- 
search. 
Decision-makers’ risk attitude signiﬁcantly impacts dual-source 
subscription planning [4 , 9 , 10] . A risk-neutral decision-maker will 
ultimately adopt the dual-sourcing strategy [10] . As the decision- 
maker becomes risk-averse, the allocation of orders to the leading 
supplier will be reduced [4] . Risk attitudes are variable and occur 
on a continuum ranging from risk avoidance through risk neutral- 
ity to risk preference. However, because the research above focuses 
on using dual-sourcing tactics from a single risk attitude, it is cru- 
cial to examine how changes in decision makers’ risk attitudes af- 
fect their purchase decisions.

Additionally, implementing economically and effectively a dual- 
sourcing strategy for supply outages is the research direction of 
many scholars [1 , 5 , 11–14] . Silbermayr and Minner [13] are con- 
cerned about all costs in the supply chain to adopt the dual-source 
ordering strategy effectively. Also, some studies on dual-sourcing 
strategy use game theory [14] or a real-option method [1] to in- 
vestigate how to effectively dual-source ordering at the risk of dis- 
ruption. However, suppose the dual-sourcing strategy is only eco- 
nomically and effectively adopted. In that case, it is often easy to 
ignore the diﬃculties in implementing the strategy caused by the 
interruption, such as uncertainty of interruption and shortage of 
demand the market will endure. As a result, we must consider how 
to eﬃciently balance costs and the scarcity of downstream demand 
in the supply chain caused by disruption, as well as how to suc- 
cessfully implement a dual-sourcing strategy in the face of various 
disruption concerns. 
Our study examines how to perform an eﬃcient and stable 
dual-source subscription program with different decision-makers 
risk attitudes under supply disruption uncertainty. First, we con- 
struct a dual-objective robust optimization model for dual-sourcing 
planning and express the uncertain interruption probability as an 
interval [3] . The conservatism parameter in a robust model, in a 
sense, indicates decision-makers’ attitudes about risk. Second, we 
apply and analyze the model using the real enterprise data. We 
conﬁrm the viability of the dual-sourcing strategy, examine the 
suggested model’s eﬃcacy in cost and market shortfall balancing, 
and investigate the model’s stability under uncertainty and the im- 
pact of the decision maker’s risk attitude on the strategy. Third, 
we discuss the feasibility and applicability of the proposed model 
and summarize the full text and prospects. The model eﬃciently 
conducts dual-sourcing planning under disruption uncertainty and 
different risk attitudes, which is relatively novel in dual-sourcing 
research. 
The rest of the paper is developed as follows. Section 2 brieﬂy 
reviews the relevant literature. Section 3 starts with the prob- 
lem statement and constructs a dual-objective robust optimiza- 
tion model for dual-sourcing strategy under the supply interrup- 
tion risk and uses the augmented ε-constraint method to deal 
with this model. Section 4 carries on the numerical example anal- 
ysis and obtains the practical and managerial insights. Further, 
Section 5 discusses the feasibility and practicability of the model 
proposed. Finally, Section 6 concludes the paper. 
2. Literature Review 
In the case of supply disruptions, dual-sourcing is a complex 
problem [3] , which is solved by considering different aspects with 
various objectives. This section reviews some research literature on 
dual-sourcing around supplier reliability, outage scenario settings, 
risk attitudes, a comparative study on objectives and robust meth- 
ods, and additional supplements on dual-sourcing with an uncer- 
tainty of supply disruptions. 
In the dual-sourcing proposed by Gupta et al. [15] , the expected 
total market output decreases with supplier reliability, affecting 
supplier choice. Manufacturers pick the lower-cost supplier over 
the higher-cost supplier in the case of two suppliers with equiv- 
alent reliability, but for two suppliers with varying reliability is 
the opposite [14] . According to speciﬁc research, such as [16] , the 
buyer can affect supply reliability by offering incentives to lower 
the risk of supply interruption, like promoting the contract’s en- 
dogenous and exogenous activities of dual procurement. Supplier 
reliability is often correlated with the probability of supply disrup- 
tions. Zhu and Fu [60] proposed that an unreliable supplier tends 
to have a greater probability of disruption. 
Shu et al. [11] showed that in a dual-sourcing model with ser- 
vice constraints, the retailer’s optimal decision is inﬂuenced by 
the joint outage probability of primary and standby suppliers. Xue 
et al. [6] used the all-or-nothing type of disruption; the supplier 
cannot provide any order once an interruption occurs. Meanwhile, 
Silbermayr and Minner [13] assumed that interrupts have on/off
cycles and that the cycles are exponentially distributed. Simul- 
taneously, much literature has set outage probability parameters. 
Schmitt and Snyder [17] set the probability of interruption and 
demonstrated that a medium probability of interruption causes 
the single-period model to underestimate the impact of inter- 
ruption risk, resulting in a signiﬁcant increase in cost. Similarly, 
Ledari et al. [18] considered two suppliers with a certain prob- 
ability of interruption. In contrast, Chen et al. [19] conjectured 
that interruption has recoverability. They deﬁned “interruption”
as the failure of suppliers to deliver orders fully, including non- 
delivery, which will initially lead to a massive loss of production 
capacity; however, the level of production capacity will eventually 
improve. 
Li and Li [9] found that in the risk-neutral case, the total order 
quantity has no relation to the procurement cost and the reliability 
of reliable and unreliable suppliers, respectively. The risk attitude 
in economics and ﬁnance is quoted here through VaR and CVaR 
modeling or utility function modeling [10] . Using the expected util- 
ity function, Giri [4] studied the ordering strategy of risk-averse 
retailers and found that an increase in risk aversion reduces the 
optimal order quantity but increases the safety inventory. Mean- 
while, Sawik [20] took conditional risk value at risk measures and 
compared risk-neutral and risk-averse solutions that optimize sup- 
ply chains’ average and worst-case performance. Furthermore, risk- 
averse decision-makers tend to adopt a more “conservative” policy 
and obtain a larger order quantity by setting appropriate service 
level restrictions [60] . 
Robust optimization is an emerging concept gradually devel- 
oped in recent decades; it is a kind of uncertain optimization 
method closely related to the robust control concepts [21–25] . 
Scenario-based and interval data robust optimization methods are 
the most widely used [26–30] . In particular, the interval robust 
optimization method can reﬂect decision makers’ risk attitude in 
managing uncertainty when it is not highly dependent on the 
occurrence probability of nominal scenarios [31–34] . Jabbarzadeh 
et al. [31] expressed a time-dependent conservative value in the in- 
terval data robustness model, based on which the decision maker’s 
risk attitude can be reﬂected. In the robust model established by 
José Alem and Morabito [32] , the risk value assumed is determined 
by the decision-makers’ risk preference. Zhu et al. [33] estab- 
lished numerous intervals based on uncertain objectives, and the 
decision-makers’ risk preferences determined the order of these in- 
tervals. 
Additionally, robust optimization is an excellent method for 
studying the inﬂuence of supply-side/demand-side uncertainty on 
the supply chain [35–41] . Jabbarzadeh et al. [36] proposed a hybrid 
robust stochastic optimization model for designing a supply chain 
resilient to supply, demand, and facility interruptions. Later, Jab- 
barzadeh et al. [35] established a robust model for common supply 
interruptions and demand changes. In addition, Mirzapour Al-e- 
hashem et al. [42] proposed a robust multi-objective mixed-integer 
nonlinear programming model to deal with demand and cost un- 
certainty. Meanwhile, Lu et al. [37] solved the problem of location 
design of reliable facilities by applying distributionally robust op- 
timization. The purpose is to minimize the expected cost with a 
given marginal probability of outages in the worst-case distribution 
while allowing the outage associated with uncertain joint distribu- 
tion. The robust models developed by Goli et al. [43] , Mirzapour 
Al-e-hashem et al. [42] , and Alinezhad et al. [44] are customer- 
focused and dual-objective planning cost-strategies in which max- 
imizing customer satisfaction involves minimizing demand short- 
age. While the dual-objective robust approach put forth by Tirko-

In Table 1 , we summarize the characteristics of procurement lit- 
erature under disruption and related models, and our paper can 
be distinguished from previous work in the following ways. First, 
in contrast to other research, we set supplier reliability to the de- 
gree of change in the probability of interruption: the more signif- 
icant the change, the less reliable the supplier. So reliability and 
outage probability fuse to better represent supply uncertainty and 
study stable dual-sourcing in uncertainty sets. Furthermore, unlike 
earlier studies on single risk attitudes, we considered the impact 
of various modiﬁcations in decision-makers’ risk attitudes on dual- 
sourcing strategies. We also perfectly captured the risk attitude us- 
ing the robust optimization method. Lastly, we suggested a reli- 
able procedure for creating a dual-objective reliable dual-sourcing 
model that reduces costs and shortages. Our approach is pretty in- 
novative in the area of dual-sourcing studies. 
The main contributions of this paper are as follows. Initially, 
a dual-objective dual-sourcing model based on a robust optimiza- 
tion method is established to minimize costs and shortages. This 
method yields optimal order allocation results that satisfy various 
uncertainty sets. In addition, the robust method identiﬁes differ- 
ent risk attitudes, making it simple to research how diverse risk 
attitudes affect the outcomes of order allocation. Ultimately, our 
proposed model is implemented in a real study case, which is ap- 
plicable and feasible.

We consider two objective functions in the model, one of which 
minimizes the total cost, and the other minimizes storage quantity. 
Total cost includes purchasing, component and production, storage, 
and product cost; they are presented in detail in Eq. (1) . The mono- 
mial in Eq. (1) is in the order of ﬁxed order price, per order cost, 
stockholding/storage cost of components, stockholding/storage cost 
of products, and product cost. Eq. (13) represents the stockhold- 
ing/storage cost of products, and Eq. (14) represents the stockhold- 
ing/storage cost of components. In Eq. (13) , the ﬁrst expression 
in max (·) represents the production cost, and the second expres- 
sion is the shortage cost. When the production quantity is non- 
negative (the shortfall quantity is equal to 0), there is no shortage, 
and Eq. (13) can reﬂect either the inventory cost or the shortage 
cost. Since the production quantity is negative, it indicates a mar- 
ket shortage. The same is true for Eq. (14) for inventory/shortage 
costs of components. Eq. (2) represents minimizing total storage 
quantity. 
Constraint (3) is the component inventory level balance for- 
mula. The starting component inventory for a given period is the 
sum of the starting component inventory for the prior period plus 
the number of purchased components less the number of compo- 
nents needed to make the product. Constraint (4) indicates the 
inventory balance constraint at the stockholding level of prod- 
ucts. Similarly, the initial stock of a product in a particular cycle 
equals the initial inventory of a product in the preceding cycle plus 
the number of purchased components minus the number of com- 
ponents required to produce products and the number of prod- 
ucts required by the market. Meanwhile, constraint (5) is sourcing 
quantity on-demand at the market; the quantity purchased at the 
time of interruption equals the market demand. Constraint (6) rep- 
resents that production quantity depends on the manufacturer’s 
production capacity and the manufacturing load rate. Constraint 
(7) states that the purchased quantity cannot exceed the manufac- 
turer’s capacity limit. Constraint (8) means that the quantity pur- 
chased plus the opening inventory cannot exceed the warehouse 
capacity of the component. And similarly, constraint (9) shows that 
the product produced plus the beginning inventory cannot exceed 
the product’s storage capacity. Constraint (10) enforces the stor- 
age constraint in every market. The shortage depends on whether 
the product inventory exceeds 0. If the product inventory is less 
than 0, it indicates a shortage of the number of items the mar- 
ket requires. On the contrary, if the product inventory exceeds 0, 
the shortage quantity is 0. Moreover, constraint (11) guarantees the 
sourcing quantity of supply i is not less than zero and takes the in- 
teger. Constraint (12) deﬁnes the value of interrupt probability to 
be between 0 and 1.

3.2.2. Objective function

lems is to obtain the most preferred solution, the Pareto-optimal 
solution, instead of the optimal solution. Generally, multi-objective 
mathematical programming problem requires the weighting and 
ε-constraint method [31 , 51] . The augmented ε-constraint method 
proposed by Mavrotas [51] provides complete implementation 
codes and an interactive approach based on the ε-constraint 
method, which not only improves the model’s solvability but also 
averts the appearance of weak Pareto-optimal.

4. Model application and analysis 
4.1. Example description 
To test the feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed robust 
model, we take an information transmission equipment manufac- 
turer’s production and rough sourcing data in China. Moreover, we 
combine some studies with setting-related parameters. The enter- 
prise should pass the requirements, procurement, production and 
delivery process, and procurement stage for each order. According 
to the production plan, this process determines the procurement 
and material types levels in a production planning cycle based on 
the order or demand of a supplier to the source material. However, 
the company is prone to supply interruption due to changes in the 
market structure and technological innovation. For this reason, this 
study designed a dual-source procurement strategy to reduce the 
cost and shortage loss caused by the interruption. 
The enterprise’s dual-sourcing problem is to obtain the pre- 
ferred solution of order assignment under the constraints of capac- 
ity, resources, and inventory. There are two available suppliers: the 
main supply and the backup supply. Related parameters are in the 
appendix, among which the main supply is relatively cheap and 
more variable on disruption, whereas the backup supply is more 
expensive and less variable on disruption. The period of sourcing 
is 12 months.

risk attitudes and four variabilities for the analysis. The degree of 
change of interruption probability is calculated as follows.

5. Discussion 
In this paper, a dual-objective robust optimization model for 
dual sourcing strategy on supply interruption risk is established 
to prove the stability advantage of the robust method and the 
economic advantage of dual-source procurement. First, we ﬁnd 
that the more prominent unreliability supplier and risk-neutral 
decision-maker can save more money by adopting the results of 
the robust technique under different interrupt variability and pol- 
icymakers’ conservative cost estimates. Second, the global opti- 
mal values of the Pareto-optimal solution under different inter- 
ruption variability, cost, and shortage weight conditions are ana- 
lyzed. Therefore, for reliable suppliers, decision-makers may attach 
more importance to the shortage minimization goal downstream 
of the supply chain, but in contrast, they attach more importance 
to the total cost goal. Finally, by comparing the Pareto solutions of 
dual-source procurement strategy and single-source procurement 
strategy, it is proved that the dual-source procurement has a pro- 
nounced effect on mitigating the interruption risk. 
To reﬂect the general applicability of the method proposed in 
this paper, we introduce the data value of He et al. [1] into our 
model and analyze its dual-sourcing advantages and robust per- 
formance. Regarding capacity limits, production costs, and inven- 
tory capacity, we set them following the paper of Doganis and 
Sarimveis [58] and Javanmard and Kianehkandi [59] . Hence, we can 
obtain the comparison value on single-sourcing and dual-sourcing 
with different variability and the cost value required to save per 
unit market shortage in Fig. 9 . Besides, the TVBP increment con- 
cerning different scenarios and different target weights relative to 
the nominal scenario is in Fig. 10 . 
Fig. 9 shows dual-source procurement under the interrupt prob- 
ability of different variability. Compared with monophyletic pro- 
curement cost savings, dual-source procurement shows an eco- 
nomic advantage. With the increase in outage probability, the cost- 
saving is relatively more, but the cost of compensating for the 
shortage is increasing. It shows that a double-source purchase has 
more economic advantage but does worse in satisfying the mar- 
ket than a monophyletic purchase under the condition of unreli- 
able suppliers. In a word, in a supply chain with the risk of supply 
interruption, obtaining the products needed for production from 
dual suppliers with different production capabilities and reliabil- 
ity is more practical. It is necessary to compensate for the market 
shortage and maximize the economic loss caused by customer sat- 
isfaction. 
Fig. 10 shows the value increment of TVBP of different scenar- 
ios and target weights relative to the certain interruption proba- 
bility. The four interruption variabilities of 5%, 10%, 15%, and 20% 
are numbered 1, 2, 3, and 4, respectively. The four risk attitudes 
are numbered 1, 2, 3, and 4, respectively. Thus 16 different sce- 
narios are obtained by combination. The cost target weights are 
0.4, 0.5, and 0.6, respectively. This ﬁgure integrates the variability, 
interruption probability, and target weight in the previous section 
to compare the robust and deterministic methods. In Fig. 10 , the 
TVBP and the cost of the target weight increase as the interrupt 
probability grow. No matter which interruption is variable, the cost 
reaches the maximum in the second risk attitude. When the unre- 
liability of suppliers is greater and decision-makers’ risk attitude 
is neutral, robust approaches yield better beneﬁts in the supply 
chain. In addition, when the weight is 0.6, the result of the ro- 
bust model is always better than that of the deterministic model. 
Therefore, the robust method in the supply chain with a dual- 
source procurement mode can better achieve enterprises’ supply 
risk management objectives from an economic perspective.

6. Conclusion and future research 
Although supply chain management frequently discusses dual- 
source procurement, it is not yet well known how an eﬃcient and 
reliable dual-source subscription program with various decision- 
makers’ risk attitudes may be created in the face of supply disrup- 
tion concerns. As a result, our paper rise in response to the proper 
time and conditions. First, we build a dual-sourcing robust opti- 
mization model to reduce costs and shortages and use the interval 
robust optimization method to obtain the robust correspondence 
mode. Using the augmented ε-constraint method, we rewrite the 
shortage objective function to obtain a single-objective function in 
the form of constraints. Additionally, we conducted reliability, risk 
attitude, dual-target, and dual-sourcing advantage analysis based 
on a real case. The model performs robustly and eﬃciently under 
different uncertainties (unreliable suppliers) and different decision- 
maker attitudes. Finally, we discuss the broad applicability and fea- 
sibility. Some ﬁndings are drawn through analysis and discussion, 
which are summarized as follows: (1) Dual-sourcing strategy based 
on a robust method takes into account of the equilibrium of cost 
and market shortage to achieve eﬃcient and successful decision- 
making, and our method can adapt to various uncertainties and 
has excellent robustness. (2) Our method is more effective at less 
reliable suppliers. (3) Though our method can make effective or- 
dering plans under different decision-maker risk attitudes, more 
eﬃcient and stable results can be achieved under a risk-neutral at- 
titude. The above conclusions also prove that it is meaningful and 
wise to consider disruption uncertainty and various risk attitudes 
as dual-sourcing research.

Future research can be extended to the entire supply chain, 
considering the beneﬁts of suppliers, the game between retail- 
ers/manufacturers and suppliers, and other perspectives to explore 
the impact of supply interruption on procurement strategies. The 
future research direction of this work can also include extending 
the proposed model for multiple types of products, more than two 
sources of supply, and more levels of supply chains. Additionally,

model is attractive, promising, and meaningful. Future research can
